A blanket to take out at night that is also a star map. Laid out with its north edge pointing
north, each constellation sits under its place in the sky, and guides around the edge show how to
turn it for the season and the hour and what your latitude can see. Two products, one per
hemisphere: cheaper than a two-sided blanket, and most people only want their own sky (RJ,
2026-09-30).

## Sketch

- **Mirror image.** A map you look down at has to be drawn the way a celestial globe shows the
  sky: mirror-reversed from a star chart you hold up. Otherwise east and west are swapped when
  it's laid out north-edge-north.
- **Layout.** A polar map centered on the celestial pole (north or south), out to about 30°
  past the equator. Around the edge, a date ring and an hour ring, planisphere-style: turn the
  blanket so tonight's date sits at the hour you're out. Printed circles mark the horizon for a
  few latitudes (say 30°, 40° and 50°), so you can see what never rises where you are.
- **Content.** Stars to about magnitude 5, sized by brightness; constellation lines and names;
  the Milky Way's outline; the brand's ovaloid as the star shape, if it reads at blanket size.
  Night-blue ground, cream stars.
- **Data rights.** Not ESA's Hipparcos or Gaia (non-commercial). Candidates: the Yale Bright Star
  Catalogue (widely treated as public domain), IAU constellation boundaries, and constellation
  lines from a source whose licence allows a commercial print. Feasibility confirms each.
- **Printer.** Gelato's API catalog has no blankets (checked 2026-10-01). Feasibility compares
  printers that do, with an API and no monthly fee: Printful and Printify both list fleece and
  sherpa throws. Check print size, resolution and whether one can be printed edge to edge.

**Open questions for RJ**

1. **How it's used.** Lying on it covers the middle of the map. Is it read sitting up, or before
   and after, with the edge guides as the part you use in the dark? Or a picnic-size blanket
   (60 x 80 in) so there's room to lie beside the map?
2. **Material.** Fleece, sherpa (printed face, plain back) or a picnic blanket with a
   water-resistant back for damp ground, if any printer offers one.
3. **Glow or no glow.** No print-on-demand printer offers glow ink as far as I know, so the map
   has to read by red flashlight. Fine?
